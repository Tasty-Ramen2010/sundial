01 Problem A · Two cameras

Glue two records together and fire seems to jump in 2012.

MODIS has watched since 2000, with 1 km pixels. VIIRS joined in 2012 with 375 m pixels, so it sees many small fires MODIS cannot. Splice the two and burning leaps upward the year VIIRS starts. The jump is the camera, not the fire.

02 Problem B · Fires follow the clock

Most fires are lit and spread in the afternoon.

They peak near 3 pm local time and are weakest before dawn. So when a satellite looks changes how many fires it counts, even with the exact same camera.

Watch the globe: each dot brightens as local afternoon sweeps over it. The ring shows local solar time, which is just UTC plus longitude ÷ 15.

03 Problem C · The old satellites are drifting

Terra and Aqua ran out of fuel. Their clocks slid.

Since about 2020 Terra has crept from a 10:30 am pass toward 9:30, when fewer fires burn. Aqua has gone from 1:30 pm toward 2:30, closer to the peak. VIIRS stays near 1:30 pm, so it is the stable reference.

Scouting result: Terra alone showed a fire decline of about −53% in Angola while VIIRS showed almost none. That decline is the satellite waking up earlier, not less fire.

04 Method · The swath trick

Measure the time-of-day effect from the satellite itself.

A MODIS image is a wide strip. Its east edge is seen about 40 minutes later in local solar time than its west edge. Compare east and west pixels of the same size and the only thing that differs is the clock. The difference in counts gives the slope: how fast detections rise per hour.

The two edges also see different lighting. Terra and Aqua fly in opposite directions, so that error flips sign; adding the two slopes cancels it: badj = bTerra + bAqua.

  1. Convert the camera. From 2012–2019 both sensors fly and the drift has not started. Fit k, "for every fire MODIS saw, VIIRS saw k", per region × season × day/night.
  2. Measure the clock. Take the swath slope b each year, so the correction follows the drift.
  3. Apply it month by month. Undo the shift back to the old 10:30 pass, then convert to VIIRS units.

nharm = k × nTerra × e−b(h − 10.5)

05 Evidence · What the scouting run showed

It worked on four regions it had never seen. It also failed twice.

"Gap closed" is the share of the fake difference between drifting Terra and steady VIIRS that the correction removed. 100% is perfect, 0% did nothing. These numbers come from an AI-built scouting run and are not final: the team rebuilds every one before showing it.

    Rules we follow: write the pass/fail rule before running a test, always run a placebo, keep the failures in the story.

      07 Why now

      The 25-year record is ending.

      Suomi NPP stopped sending data on 2 November 2026. Terra and Aqua are expected to shut down around late 2026 or early 2027. Anyone who wants one long fire record has to harmonize it now.

      Who it is for

      Anyone comparing this season with the past.

      Fire managers, scientists studying fire trends, air-quality forecasters (in Delhi, fire counts help explain winter haze) and people who live near burning land.

      Honest notes

      What we do not claim.

      Add Terra and Aqua together and their drifts mostly cancel, leaving only −1% to +3% error. The fix matters most for single-satellite users and the Terra-only years of 2000–2002. AI helped with early scouting and with this site's code; we rebuild the analysis and check every source ourselves.
